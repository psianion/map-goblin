What a DM sees at 1280×720. The rail is a single 256px column that scrolls; about a fifth of it is on screen at once (the whole rail is unrolled on the right). The only surfaces over the map are the fit button, the tool indicator, and the status bar. Prompts (initiative, traps) and toasts stack above the status bar when they fire.

Player seat

60 FPS·16.6ms·Connected2 ms·Night · Full moon · Dusk4%

What a player sees ("Willow"). Six panels, ~1,100px. No tool indicator, no fit-button difference, same status bar (FPS and frame-time included). Clicking a token name selects it and offers Claim; clicking a door name re-frames the camera and offers Open/Close. Everything else is read-only.

A
Players get the DM's layout minus panels, not a player layout. Same column, same heading style, same order; Initiative on top only because it sorts first. Nothing answers "whose turn is it / where am I / what can I do" at a glance.
B
Door and token lists duplicate the map. Both are things the player can already see and click on the canvas; the list is the only way to act on them today, so the eye has to leave the map.DoorPanel.tsx:37, TokenPanel.tsx:302.
C
Posting a roll is a text box. "stealth 17" in a 200-char input; the composer is the last thing in the rail, below two scroll boxes, so on a laptop it's off-screen until scrolled to.GameLog.tsx:194.
D
FPS / frame-time are shown to players. Dev telemetry in the player's status bar; the env badge ("Night · Full moon · Dusk") is the only line that speaks to the game.TableStatusBar.tsx:106-189.
