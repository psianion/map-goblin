Table icons, four ways to draw them

Same nine glyphs, same 24px grid, drawn from scratch for Good Goblin (crossed blades, drifting mist, an arched door, a pawn, a folded map, a crescent, a pressure plate going off, a quill over a page, a portrait). What changes between the options is the hand: line weight, caps, and whether shapes are outlined, filled, or inked. Each is shown in the rail at real size, enlarged, and as inline button glyphs. Pick the hand; M2 then draws the full set (10 rail + ~12 inline) in it.

A

Fine ink

1.5px single-weight line, round caps, full detail kept (quill barbs, spark flecks, map folds). Closest to the editor's current chrome; quietest on the rail.

  • Reads at 20px, but the thinnest option on a dim panel.
  • Detail survives best at 40px; least character at 20px.
B

Bold ink

2.6px line, square caps, small details dropped. The "thick dark stroke" the art guide uses for walls, carried into the chrome.

  • Strongest silhouette at 20px; the rail reads from across the room.
  • Fewer interior lines, so each glyph has to earn its shape.
C

Stamp

Solid shapes with negative-space detail, like a rubber stamp or a woodcut. Fills on closed forms, heavy line on open ones.

  • Most "object-like": the door is a door, the pawn is a pawn.
  • Heaviest on the rail; needs the most care so active green doesn't shout.
D

Inked fill

1.75px outline with a muted wash inside closed shapes, the way the door and token sprites are outlined over a painted fill.

  • Sits between A and C: silhouette of a fill, lightness of a line.
  • The wash takes the accent on the active icon, so state reads in two ways.

Geometry here is a first draft to judge the hand, not final art. Whichever you pick, M2 redraws every glyph properly in that hand, adds the inline set (reveal, hide, brush, lock, secret, frame, place, copy, close, more, plus, whisper, dice), and comes back on this page for sign-off before anything is wired into the rail.
