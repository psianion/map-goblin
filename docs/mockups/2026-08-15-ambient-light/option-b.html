Ambient light — Option B: Sky ribbon

The interpolated day is the interface. One ribbon component carries the whole feature on both surfaces: in the Editor it is simultaneously the palette editor (five keyframe swatches sit on it at their own hours and are the recolour handles) and the local day-scrub; at the Table it is the campaign clock, read-mostly, with a playhead and quick-jump notches. The DM learns one object once. The ribbon paints in map-world colour only — the moss accent and the Table's white stay on selection and state, never on the world's mood.

1 · The ribbon — one component, one grammar

Everything below reuses these five parts, so no later mockup has to re-explain itself. The hero is live: drag the playhead (or focus it and use the arrow keys) and switch the sky. The gradient is a single CSS interpolation in Ok-space — the same continuous blend the plan specifies for the grade, so scrubbing can never look stepped.

2 · Editor — “Environment”, map properties panel

A CollapsibleSection in the 300px properties overlay, appended beside GridSection/AmbientSection, in the Moss night tokens. Two strips do the heavy lifting: the palette ribbon (authored, recolourable) and, under it, the applied strip — what this map actually receives once environment damping and the mood tint are composed in. The applied strip is the answer to “what does the ribbon mean when the clock is damped or ignored”: the palette stays authored and intact, the applied strip tells the truth.

3 · Table — “World” block, Session Controls (DM only)

Same ribbon, Table dress: bigger type, achromatic tokens, a solid playhead, and no keyframes — at the table the palette is settled and the DM only moves time. Below it the block splits cleanly in two: the world's look (clock, sky, speed) and the vision gate (auto value, override, effective badge). That split is the point — an override takes the gate, never the grade, so the world still looks like the hour it is.

Status-bar mirror keeps today's “omit the boring” rule: at Daylight · Auto the bottom bar shows nothing new; it prints Night · Crescent · Darkness or Darkness (override) only when the world is doing something. The World block's badge is always present because it is the thing a DM opens the panel to read.

4 · On a dressed map — Fieldstone Keep

Grade is a multiply under the light pools, never over them: the braziers stay warm at every hour, which is the style-guide's “every light source keeps its warm glow as the ambient grade shifts”. Shadows extrude one way per view (annotated), and they vanish entirely under a moonless sky. Chrome sits beside the art, quiet — the colour on screen belongs to the world.

What it costs to build

  • One new component, used twice. <DayRibbon> — track, keys, playhead, ticks — with an editable flag. Editor mounts it with keys and the preview head; Table mounts it read-mostly with the live head. The rest is existing vocabulary: CollapsibleSection, PropertyField, ColorField, ToggleSwitch in the Editor; Segmented/Switch lifted out of FogTool.tsx into a shared file at the Table (this is their second consumer).
  • The gradient is free. linear-gradient(90deg in oklab, …) renders the interpolation the resolver already computes — no canvas, no sampling, no per-frame work. The ribbon cannot drift from the engine because both read the same five keyframes.
  • The compass is the only true from-scratch control (nothing angle-shaped exists in either app): 52px SVG dial, pointer-drag + arrow keys, snap to 15° with Shift.
  • Commit idiom unchanged. Keyframe drag → store live, release → SetTimeKeyframeCommand; compass drag identical. Table controls ride the set-environment optimistic-echo path with its 4 s fallback.
  • Badge is a component, not a string. Level glyph + word + provenance chip; the status bar keeps its own comma-joined summary and its omit-the-default rule.
  • Risk to watch: at 232px the Table ribbon is ~13px of track per hour. Fine for jumping and scrubbing; not fine for setting an exact minute, so the numeric readout is the precise control and quick jumps do the rest.
